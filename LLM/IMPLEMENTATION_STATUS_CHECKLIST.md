## How to record evidence

Keep new records in **Current evidence**, before the historical block. Update
the affected navigation row when a gate gains or loses relevant evidence;
retain superseded results below instead of rewriting their outcome.

A bounded record contains:

- Changed contract and scope, including save/migration impact when relevant.
- Command or actual human/browser action, result, and evidence artifact.
- Input/build identity for expensive or concurrent runs; note observed drift.
- What it proves and what remains outside that run.

Use explicit evidence labels: **source checked**, **automated pass/fail**,
**browser observed**, **human accepted**, or **not run**. Include the date and
scope of an observed result, not a blanket file-wide freshness date. Keep gate
state separate from implementation progress. Never infer a full pass from a
focused retry or a historical green row.

### 2026-09-24 — Water overhaul: smooth, stable plane-wave sea, LOD lattice, shared baked field, river flow map and fall (presentation only)

- **Changed contract (presentation only; no save, schema, layout or gameplay change):** the uniform coarse water grid, its culling tiles and the High-tier near patch are replaced by a camera-centred CDLOD lattice (`WaterLod.ts`: two instanced draws, crack-free morphing) plus a row-refined surface for the elevated headwater reach. The wave field (`WaterSurface.ts`/`waveGlsl.ts`) is three Gerstner plane-wave bands (swell, cross-swell, wind sea) with a fixed wave vector, speed and heading each; only amplitude follows region, depth and sea state, so a turning wind no longer swings the sea and regional blends no longer shear it over time. Shoaling, a smooth depth-limited breaking cap and swash come from one baked field (`waterField.ts`) read by every shader and by the CPU mirror, so buoyancy samples the drawn surface. Shading is smooth rather than faceted: the interpolated analytic normal plus a procedural tileable detail normal map (`WaterDetailNormals.ts`) in world-aligned texture space (two drifting sea layers, a bounded two-phase flow map on rivers), Toksvig slope variance, the atmosphere's sky probe (`AtmosphereSky.reflectionTarget`), a GGX glint, SSR/refraction on High and Voronoi caustics. Foam is capped and broken: surf lines on the swell crests with the energy saturated before crest gathering, scattered whitecaps only in rough water, hull lace, object contact only in rough water, and a broken wake trail. Rough water is turbid (`optics.roughTurbidity`), so the faceted seabed no longer shows through deep channel water in a storm. The fall sheet shares ribs, streaked detail and the probe reflection. `ShoreFoam` keeps only patch placement; `waterCausticsGlsl.ts` is removed. `VisualRenderConfig` gains `waterSurface.lod`, `detailNormals`, `optics.roughTurbidity`, `whitecap.scatter`, fall `detail` and `atmosphere.reflectionProbe`, and drops the facet, ripple-normal, flow-normal and shoreline tuning.

### Physics query cost — profiled and reduced, 2026-09-06

A profile of the physics layer, rather than a reading of it, found the cost was almost entirely in redundant queries.

- **`hasLineOfSight` cost 6.5 ms per call — roughly 40% of a 60 Hz frame.** `world.projectPoint` was 99.5% of it, and isolating the cause showed the two terrain heightfield colliders were the whole of that: excluding them took the query from 6.50 ms to 0.0097 ms. `colliderBelongsToEndpoint` already rejects terrain, so the projection was computed and discarded every time. The query now filters terrain by collider handle, which cannot change the result. `hasLineOfSight` measures 0.035 ms. `InteractionTargetResolver` calls this once per line-of-sight candidate and `evaluateInteractionTarget` runs at up to 15 Hz, so this was several milliseconds of avoidable work per second whenever an NPC was a candidate.
- **The physics step was about 94% layout sampling.** Instrumented call counting showed 6.1 `traversalSurfaceSample` calls per on-foot step and zero height-only calls, at roughly 0.154 ms each. Five of the six discarded the normal that `traversalSurfaceSample` builds from four extra neighbour samples. `traversalSurfaceHeight` now takes the raw sample directly instead of going through the full one — the sampled height is identical by construction — and the step's height-only queries use it. The resolved point, the one place a normal is genuinely needed, is sampled once and shared between the ground height and the contact evidence. Per-step layout work: **6.1 full samples → 4 height-only plus 1 full**, about 0.79 ms to 0.32 ms.
- **`terrainSurface` derived a normal the caller already held.** `terrainSurfaceSample` has always accepted `sampledNormalY`; `terrainSurface` did not forward one, so every contact-surface classification paid four extra terrain height samples. It now accepts and forwards it, and the physics step passes the normal from the sample it just took. Cost falls from 0.400 ms to 0.046 ms. Over 11,643 sampled terrain and road points the two normals pick a different material at **2 points (0.017%)**, both sitting exactly on the grass/cliff slope threshold; the traversal normal describes the surface actually under the capsule, so it is the better evidence at those points.
- A 64-entry direct-mapped memo for the raw traversal sample was implemented, measured and **removed**: consecutive queries are never at identical coordinates, so it added hashing and store cost without hits.

Evidence and its limits. Ratios were measured inside a single process so both variants share machine conditions: height-only query 3.9x, `terrainSurface` with a known normal 4.9x, `projectPoint` 310x under load, and 4.2x / 8.6x / 670x in a quieter run. Absolute milliseconds moved by a factor of four to six between runs because another session was working in this tree throughout, so **no wall-clock or end-to-end claim is made** — the reported figures are single-process A/B ratios and noise-free call counts, not browser frame times. `tests/simulation/physicsTraversal.test.ts` did drop from 11.1 s to 4.5 s on the same tests, which corroborates the step reduction without proving a frame-rate figure. Correctness: **82 tests in 10 files** pass (physics, traversal, mounts, edge cases, collision debug, terrain snapping, harbor coast, terrain and road surface material), and **72 tests in 4 files** across persistence and all three terrain migrations. ESLint reports zero problems and no changed file produces a TypeScript error.

## Lighting, atmosphere and canopy-wind pass — 2026-09-04

Renderer-baseline change across the whole day cycle. `VisualRenderConfig` owns
every number; `LLM/04_ART_DIRECTION_BIBLE_PREMIUM_COZY_LOW_POLY.md` sections 7,
7.1, 9 and 16 own the rules. Five changes:

- **Golden hour.** The key's daylight ramp reached zero just below the horizon, so
  the sun switched off exactly when it was lowest and warmest and dawn/dusk were
  a flat ambient lift. The ramp now carries the key across the horizon
  (`sun.daylightFullSolarHeight` / `daylightZeroSolarHeight`) and one
  horizon-proximity envelope (`twilight.solarWidth`) drives both the warm sky band
  and the key colour, which is anchored on the day key so noon is unchanged. New
  `horizon_gold_01` sky token.
- **Shadow range.** `shadows.intensity` is no longer fully opaque, which was
  crushing dark palette families to flat black. `shadows.nightIntensity` /
  `nightRadius` give the moon its own lighter, broader recipe.
- **Night key-to-fill.** Night ran fill-dominant (moon 0.92 : fill 0.62) against
  day's 3:1, so moonlit forms had no lit side. Now key-dominant at roughly
  constant total night brightness.
- **Emissive response.** `bloom` and `grade` were declared and read by nothing.
  Emissive palette materials now scale with the practical-light envelope, so lit
  windows fade up at dusk instead of glowing at noon, and practical lights carry
  additive glow sprites capped by `practicalLightBudget` rather than a fullscreen
  bloom pass the low tier cannot afford.
- **Canopy wind.** Trees were the only static thing in a moving frame. Sway is a
  vertex offset inside the existing shared vegetation variant material.

Village dwellings (`house_cottage_a/b/c`, `building_inn_a/b`) gained a
`*_lantern_glow` node through the existing `preserve_names` mechanism, reusing the
palette emissive they already carried so no asset gained a material; four lamp
posts were added along village and orchard routes. Market halls, barns, sheds and
outhouses are deliberately excluded — see `VILLAGE_LANTERN_VARIANTS`. Of those
dwellings only `house_cottage_a` still ships; the `_b`/`_c` cottages and both inn
variants were retired when the authored village buildings replaced them.

Evidence: `tests/unit/lightingRig.test.ts`, `rendererFoundation.test.ts`,
`practicalLights.test.ts` and the new `tests/unit/vegetationWind.test.ts` pass;
`npm run typecheck` and `eslint` on the touched files are clean; the five
regenerated GLBs publish with their glow nodes and hash-match the manifest.

Performance was measured, not assumed. `npm run test:budget` reports 588 draw
calls / 3,044,983 triangles with this pass enabled and 595 / 3,048,971 with its
render additions disabled — identical within frame-sampling noise, so this change
